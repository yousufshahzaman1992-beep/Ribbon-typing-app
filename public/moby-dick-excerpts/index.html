Why long passages matter

Anyone can hold speed for a sentence. A timed test asks you to hold it for minutes. Long passages expose where your rhythm collapses and give you a chance to practise recovering without stopping.

  • Builds stamina for multi-minute typing tests.
  • Trains punctuation in dense, clause-heavy sentences.
  • Teaches you to keep going after a mistake.

How to practise without burning out

Split the excerpt

Do one short section at a time instead of the whole passage.

Keep a steady pace

A sustainable rhythm beats bursts followed by stalls.

Type through errors

Do not stop for every mistake; finish the section first.

Review afterwards

List the words that broke your rhythm and repeat them.

Reading ahead

Long sentences reward readers who look ahead. Keep your eyes a few words in front of your fingers so punctuation and difficult vocabulary do not interrupt the flow.

If a passage feels exhausting, shorten the section rather than lowering your standards.

Frequently asked questions

Is Moby Dick public domain?

Yes, Moby Dick is public domain in most countries and is widely used for typing practice.

Are long excerpts suitable for beginners?

Beginners should start with shorter sections and gradually increase length as rhythm improves.

What does a long excerpt train that drills do not?

Endurance, sustained rhythm and the ability to recover from mistakes without stopping.
