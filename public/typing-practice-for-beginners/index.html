Typing Practice for Beginners: Learn the Home Row

If you still hunt for letters, the fastest way forward is not to type faster — it is to type correctly, slowly, until your fingers know where the keys are. This guide shows you exactly where to start, and Ribbon's first lessons walk you through it key by key.

Start here: the home row

Touch typing begins at the home row. Your index fingers rest on F and J, which have small raised bumps so you can find them without looking. From there:

  • Left hand: pinky on A, ring on S, middle on D, index on F.
  • Right hand: index on J, middle on K, ring on L, pinky on ; (semicolon).
  • Thumbs: both hover over the space bar.

Every other key is reached from this rest position, and every finger returns home after each press. That "return home" habit is the whole trick. Ribbon's beginner lessons are pure home-row drills with an on-screen hand guide, so you always know which finger is next.

Five rules that make beginners improve fast

  1. Accuracy first, speed second. A run at 20 WPM with 100% accuracy builds skill; a run at 40 WPM with 80% accuracy builds bad habits. Ribbon scores accuracy and speed together for this reason.
  2. Never look at your hands. Use the virtual keyboard and hand guide instead. Looking down feels faster today but slows you down permanently.
  3. Keep your fingers on the home row. Resist the temptation to "park" your hand over the keys you are about to hit.
  4. Practise in short, frequent sessions. Ten focused minutes a day beats a single one-hour session each week.
  5. Track one number per week. Let on-screen analytics show your WPM and accuracy trend so progress feels real.

Common beginner mistakes (and the fix)

Peeking at the keyboard

Fix: hide your hands, use the on-screen guide, and accept a temporary slowdown.

Chasing speed too early

Fix: cap runs at a pace where you can stay above 95% accuracy, then let speed follow.

Using the wrong fingers

Fix: follow a fixed finger map. Consistent fingers create consistent muscle memory.

Practising only words you know

Fix: drill the specific keys and bigrams you actually mistype, not whatever feels comfortable.

Build your first 30 days

A simple routine works best. On day one, do only home-row drills until you can finish a few lines without looking. Add one new key group every few days. Once the alphabet feels stable, move to short words, then full sentences, then a 60-second timed test to see where you stand. Frequently asked questions

How do I start typing if I have never touch typed before?

Start with the home row. Rest your fingers on A S D F and J K L ; and keep them there. Ribbon's first lessons are pure home-row drills with an on-screen hand guide, so you learn the rest position before adding reaches.

What is a good typing speed for a beginner?

Most people start between 15 and 30 words per minute. With short daily practice, 40 WPM is a realistic first milestone and 60 WPM is a comfortable working speed. Accuracy matters more than raw speed early on.

How long does it take to learn to touch type?

The home row becomes automatic within a week or two of short daily practice. Comfortable touch typing across the whole keyboard usually takes one to three months, depending on how consistently you practise.

Should I look at the keyboard while learning?

Try not to. Ribbon shows a virtual keyboard and a hand guide so you can find the next key without looking down. Looking away is what builds the muscle memory that eventually makes you faster.
